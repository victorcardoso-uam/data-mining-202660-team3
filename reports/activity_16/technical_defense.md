# Best-Fit Model: Model 5

We selected Model 5 as the best-performing candidate in this experiment. It uses discharge current, internal resistance, ambient temperature, coolant flow, and state of charge to predict battery cell temperature. It achieved a test R2 of 0.9835, a test MAE of 1.1355 C, and a test RMSE of 1.3936 C. These were the best test results among the five candidates. Its adjusted training R2 also increased from 0.9731 in Model 4 to 0.9811, supporting the inclusion of the fifth predictor.

Adding state of charge reduced the test RMSE from 1.6962 C to 1.3936 C and the test MAE from 1.3574 C to 1.1355 C. Since this variable is already available in our dataset, Model 5 provides better predictive performance without requiring an additional data source for this experiment. However, we did not measure sensor, integration, or maintenance costs. Therefore, we cannot claim a financial return on investment. Model 4 remains a reasonable alternative if state-of-charge measurements are unavailable or costly to maintain.

The Model 5 residual plot shows points on both sides of zero, without a clear curved pattern or a pronounced funnel shape. Its normal probability plot follows the reference line closely, with small deviations. These visual findings support approximate linearity, constant error variance, and residual normality, but they do not prove that all assumptions hold. Error independence was not assessed. The conclusion is limited to the five candidate models and the shared 80/20 split used in this activity; further validation would be needed before production deployment.
